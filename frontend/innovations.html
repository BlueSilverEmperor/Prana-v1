Innovations

1

An attacker that learns, built in

Vero attacks itself thousands of times so new tricks are found before a real attacker finds them.

The problem

Security rules are usually tested only against attacks people already thought of. A new combination slips through.

How Vero solves it

  1. A Markov chain Monte Carlo search starts from known attacks.
  2. Each step changes one detail: amount, recipient, method, nesting, padding.
  3. It keeps the changes that get closest to fooling the rules, and reports anything that gets through.

2

Attacks hidden inside batches

Splitting a drain into small pieces, or hiding it behind a real payment, does not fool Vero.

The problem

Checks that look at one call at a time can be beaten by cutting a big theft into several small calls that each look fine.

How Vero solves it

  1. An adaptive attacker builds batches from live accounts: slices just over the cap, skims behind real payments, lookalikes, dust trails. After every block it tries something sneakier.
  2. Vero adds up every outflow and every allowance per recipient across the whole batch.
  3. The total is held to what the signer declared. The red team found a real gap here, and it is now closed.

      3

      An immune system shared by every vault

      When one vault blocks an attacker, every other vault becomes immune to that attacker.

      The problem

      After an exchange is attacked, other teams wait days for a post-mortem before they protect themselves. The attacker moves on to the next target meanwhile.

      How Vero solves it

      1. A blocked attack reports the address it relied on (never a token contract, never the vault's own contacts).
      2. When 2 different vaults report the same address, it becomes a vaccine.
      3. Rule R12 then blocks that address for every vault, even ones it never touched.

        4

        The same six words on every device

        Your laptop, your phone and the contract compute the same six words for a transaction, so a hacked screen cannot hide a different one.

        The problem

        If the screen you sign on is compromised, everything it shows can be faked. You need a second, independent check that is quick to compare.

        How Vero solves it

        1. The chain, vault, nonce, transaction hash and intent hash are hashed together.
        2. The hash is cut into six 11-bit pieces, each one a word from the standard 2,048-word list.
        3. The API, the guard SDK and the contract use the exact same recipe, pinned by the same test in Solidity and Python.
